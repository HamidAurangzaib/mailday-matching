Member Lifecycle Map

Every path a member can travel — and the work behind each step.

Built for Courtney and anyone new joining the team. Last updated May 2026.

This map answers one question: what happens to a family, when, and who does it? If you are new to MailDay, this is the place to start — it shows the whole business as the journey one family takes through it.

Quick context: MailDay matches kids with pen pals and mails them monthly themed packs — real letters, no screens. A parent subscribes, we match their child with another child by age and shared interests, and letters start crossing the country. This map traces that journey from sign-up to… wherever it ends.

It comes in two views. The Spine — everyone passes through it: subscribe, onboard, queue, match, active. The Branches — what happens after a match goes live, the five paths a member can fall into, and how they all resolve.

Click any box on the map to see exactly what that step involves — who does it, what to send, what happens next.

The guarantee clock on a dissolved match

When a match ends — rematch, a cancelled partner, or a tier mismatch — the orphaned child re-enters the queue and the 21-day clock resets to day 0. A child who already waited a full cycle and then lost a pen pal is not penalized for it.

The win-back trigger and sequence for a ghosting family

Ghosting is defined as two consecutive monthly pack emails opened by neither — roughly a 45-day window, since packs send on the 1st. Newsletter opens don't count; the pack email is the product. When a family hits that threshold, Klaviyo automatically starts the win-back email sequence and flags the family at-risk in the app. If the sequence ends with the family still disengaged, the app creates a task and Courtney mails one handwritten Poppy card. Only two human touches in the whole branch: mailing the card and marking it done.

Open gaps — the amber boxes

Each amber box is a question for the developer. When the audit comes back, every one should resolve to "confirmed" or "built."

  1. Incomplete onboarding — a paid family that never finished the form must be nudged, then surface in Action Items.
  2. Address unconfirmed — a matched child with no confirmed address needs its own visible status.
  3. At-risk & win-back — settled. Klaviyo fires the win-back on two missed pack emails. The app must receive the at-risk flag from Klaviyo and create the Poppy-card task at the end of the sequence.
  4. Win-back execution — the email sequence and the Poppy-card trigger both need to be built.
  5. Tier mismatch flag — a tier change that leaves an active match cross-tier must flag the pair.
  6. Orphaned partner — every dissolved match must route the remaining child through the shared re-queue path.
  7. Data deletion — COPPA-compliant hard delete including match history; admin-only and logged.
